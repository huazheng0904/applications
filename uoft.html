Programs to apply to

These four are ranked in the ESA under one OUAC choice, Engineering Science (TK), listed with Machine Intelligence as the major of interest and PEY co-op.

ESA rankProgramCompetitive range*Why
1Engineering ScienceLow to mid 90sTwo broad years, then a major: Engineering Physics; Math, Statistics & Finance; Machine Intelligence; Energy Systems; and others
2Electrical EngineeringLow to mid 90sPhysics-heavy: electronics, energy systems, photonics, signal processing and ML
3Industrial EngineeringHigh 80s to low 90sStatistics, operations research and data analytics; the realistic backup
4Civil or Mechanical (optional)High 80s to low 90sCivil for water and environment; Mechanical for physics

*% average in prerequisite courses. TrackOne (undeclared engineering) is paused for 2027 entry.

Second U of T choice

Mathematical & Physical Sciences, St. George (TPG): applied math, physics, statistics or math, chosen after first year; mid to high 80s; no supplement. College preference: none selected, so U of T assigns one.

Co-op

U of T co-op is one long paid placement, not Waterloo-style rotating terms. Engineering students apply through the program; it does not affect admission.

ProgramRouteLength and timingDetails
All EngineeringPEY Co-op (Professional Experience Year)12–16 months, full-time, after 3rd yearAbout 70% of 3rd-year engineers take it; average pay about $50,000 a year; 350+ employers; 12 months count toward the P.Eng. licence
All EngineeringSummer internship (ESIP)About 4 months, after 2nd or 3rd yearOptional; with PEY, up to about 20 months of paid work
Arts & Science Math & Physical SciencesArts & Science Co-op (by major)4–16 months, after 2nd and 3rd yearSelected majors only

Sample PEY employers: NVIDIA, Intel, Amazon, IBM, Google, Environment and Climate Change Canada.

The Personal Profile allows one attempt only. Practice questions are unlimited, so finish them before the real sitting.

Personal Profile: how to prepare

Three randomly selected, timed video questions on the Kira Talent platform, about 30 minutes in one sitting. There is one official attempt and no retake, even for technical problems; practice questions are unlimited. The committee assesses the content of the answers (reasoning and communication), not how the applicant looks on camera, and there is no single right answer.

WhenStep
Nov 12–16Build 5–6 short stories to adapt: a project led, a team commitment, helping others, a failure or change of mind, and why engineering (and why Engineering Science)
Nov 17–21Practise each story in four beats: situation in one sentence, what he did, what happened, what he learned. Use specifics: numbers, names, decisions
Nov 17–22Run the Kira practice questions repeatedly to learn the real timing; finish with a clear point rather than mid-sentence
Nov 22Mock run: someone asks three unseen questions with a 1–2 minute timer; record and watch it back once
Nov 23–25Real Personal Profile on a quiet weekday evening, after a final technology check
  • Setup: laptop or desktop with webcam and microphone, a Kira-supported browser, stable internet, a quiet room, pen and paper
  • Rules: no one else present in person or online; no other apps or devices; type any text directly into Kira, never paste
  • On the day: look at the camera, keep notes to keywords, link an unexpected question to the closest story in the first sentence; do not memorise scripts
  • If the internet or power fails, report it at kiratalent.com/support immediately and email the Engineering admissions office

One attempt only: the real assessment starts at "Start Assessment". Finish the practice questions first.
